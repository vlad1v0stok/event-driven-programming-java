# Week 2 - Final Reflection

The Observer pattern defines a one-to-many relationship in which a subject keeps a list of observers and automatically notifies all of them whenever its state changes. It is important because it removes direct dependencies between the object that changes and the objects that react, so new behaviour can be added just by registering another observer, as I did with `TextLogger` without editing `DataModel`. It is the foundation of event-driven programming: an event source such as a `JButton` is a subject, its `ActionListener`s are observers, and clicking the button is the state change that triggers the notification. Building the Weather Station from scratch showed me what Swing and JavaFX are doing behind the scenes every time I add a listener. Comparing the mechanisms showed why the legacy `java.util.Observable` was deprecated, with its forced inheritance and the silent `setChanged()` trap. For my own project I would choose a custom Observer interface in plain Java, because it is type-safe, needs no inheritance and gives me full control, and I would switch to JavaFX Properties and Bindings for a JavaFX application, where they do the same job with less code.
